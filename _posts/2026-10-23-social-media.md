I'm going to try and convince you to delete Instagram. Firstly, ask yourself: Why do you use Instagram? You might want to get out some paper and write down a couple of reasons. Once you're done, come back here.

You might have reasons resembling the following:

- Keeping up to date with friends
- Sharing funny reels/posts
- Communication (text messages, group calls, etc)

Communication is clearly important. However, this is not what makes Instagram unique. Instagram began as a basic app to share photos, allowing people to see each other's best moments.

Such a simple concept right? How could things possibly go wrong?

A big part of instagram is engagement - likes, comments, views. Any post you make has a like counter. More engagement = More likes 

Let's say you and your friends both have 500 followers. You both go on separate vacations and then post about it afterwards. Your post gets 10 likes and no comments, but your friend gets 400 likes and over 100 people commented on it!

How would that make you feel? Pretty gutted right? If this was me I would probably have thoughts along the lines of:

Why did person X not like my post? Why did I only get 10 likes? Do people not like me? 

Humans love figures because numbers = information. More likes = good, less likes = bad. This subtle pressure to get more likes can be damaging. You might catch yourself constantly checking the like counter of your recent post/story, or checking who has liked it or seen it. Eventually you might find yourself almost craving the feeling of likes because of the sense of validation it gives you. The idea that you only good enough or popular enough if you get a certain amount of likes and engagement on your posts.

But that's not all. You also see the posts made by the (potentially 100s of) people you follow. While you go about your daily life, you are constantly reminded about someone's cool vacation, someone's amazing achievement, someone's birthday party, someone's concert. 

Everyday you are being bombarded by the best moments of everyone you know. What does your brain make of all this? How does your perceived life compare to the perceived lives of everyone else? 

Terrible. While your on the way to work, someone is chilling in the Bahamas, someone is at an Ariana Grande concert, someone is dunebiking in Morocco. 

It can be too easy for the brain to make these subtle comparisons. Social media offers an extremely biased lense into everyone else's life, giving the illusion that your life sucks. After all, why even live life when you can live through the best moments of other people?

It makes you wonder what life was like for humans before the Internet. Has social media been an overall benefit for humans? This is something I hope to understand better.
